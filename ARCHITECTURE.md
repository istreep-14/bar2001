# Architecture

## Shape
- **Server:** Node 22, built-ins only. `node:http` for the API, `node:sqlite` for storage, `node:test` for tests.
- **Storage:** one SQLite file (`DB_PATH`, WAL mode, foreign keys on). The whole schema is `server/schema.sql`, applied when a database is first created. Migrations are written only when the data is worth carrying over (`MIGRATIONS` in `server/db.js`, keyed by the version each upgrades from). An older file with a full chain of them is copied to `<name>.v<version>-<time>.bak` (`VACUUM INTO`, so it is consistent), then upgraded in place, one transaction per step, with a foreign-key check at the end. Upgrading (migrating or setting aside) runs under a `<db>.migrating` lock file, so several processes starting together (two dev watchers, say) take turns and the later ones find the work done; a test starts three at once. Another test proves a migrated database has the same tables, columns and indexes as a fresh one. A file with no path to the current version is renamed aside to `<name>.v<version>-<time>.db` (with its `-wal`/`-shm`), never modified or deleted, and a fresh database is started in its place. Bump `SCHEMA_VERSION` whenever `schema.sql` changes incompatibly.
- **Host-agnostic:** everything is configured by env (`PORT`, `HOST`, `DB_PATH`, `API_TOKEN`). Moving from the Mac to a cloud host is a deploy, not a rewrite. It needs Node ≥ 22.13 and a persistent volume for the DB file.

## Invariants (don't break these)
1. **Store inputs only.** Paid time, estimated wage, $/hr, per-day and per-night totals and averages are derived at read time and never written. (`shift_type` and the breaks are inputs the user chooses, so they are stored.) The derived numbers are a shift's `derived` block and `GET /employees/summary` (below). A shift's block, from the pure `server/pay.js`: `paid_minutes` ("time worked"), `wage_rate_cents`, `estimated_wage_cents`, `tips_cents`, `other_income_cents`, `total_income_cents`, and `tips_per_hour_cents`, `other_per_hour_cents` and `total_per_hour_cents`; and the staffing and party figures `bartender_count`, `bartender_minutes`, `staff_tips_cents`, `staff_tips_per_bartender_hour_cents`, `has_party` and `party_count`. Tips are the entries of the built-in Tips type (a fixed id, so no lookup); other is every other entry; the total is tips + other + the wage when there is one; each per-hour figure is its amount over paid time, to the cent, and null with no paid time. The parts (a *bartender* is you, plus each employee on the shift whose role is blank or "Bartender"; their hours are their own start to end, or nothing if none was given; staff tips are yours plus every employee's, whatever their role): **paid time** is the shift's length minus its breaks (breaks are unpaid); the **rate** is the wage-history row with the latest `effective_from` on or before the shift's `work_date`; the **wage** is paid time × rate, rounded to the cent, and `null` when no rate applies yet. It is added to every shift the API returns, recomputed each time (so changing a rate re-prices every shift at once), is not accepted on write, and is never a column. The employee summary is per person: `shifts`, `timed_shifts`, `minutes` (their own start to end, where given), `tipped_shifts`, `tips_cents`, `first_worked` and `last_worked`, over shifts that are not deleted, read from `shift_employees` on every request.
2. **Money is integer cents**, never negative. Every entry has a type (an `income_categories` row). **Tips** is built in, at a fixed id, and is what an entry with no type means; it can't be renamed, archived or removed.
3. **Times are wall-clock, with no timezone.** A time is a local `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM` string: the clock on the wall where the shift was worked. `end_at > start_at`, a shift is at most 24h, and an overnight shift is simply an end on the next date. Zoned times (`Z` or an offset) are rejected. Durations are wall-clock differences, so the two nights a year the clocks change are off by an hour; that was judged irrelevant.
4. **Every shift has a `shift_type`: `day`, `night` or `double`.** It is required and is never guessed, so a shift is always established as one of the three for later analysis.
5. **A shift has zero to many breaks; each is either a time range or a duration, never both.** A range (`start_at` + `end_at`) must sit inside the shift, ranges can't overlap, and all the breaks together (ranges and durations) can't be longer than the shift. The schema's CHECKs cover the shape of one break; the rest spans tables, so the validator owns it.
6. **Income follows the shift type.** On a day or night shift every income entry, of any type, belongs to that type (`part` is filled in for you, and can't be the other one). On a double each entry is for the `day`, the `night`, or is combined / not sure (`part: null`).
7. **A shift is identified by a client-generated UUID**, never by its date, so double shifts recorded as two shifts, or a day and a night on one date, are ordinary. `PUT /shifts/:id` is create-or-replace, so retries are safe.
8. **One writer per concern.** Only `server/catalog.js` writes wage rates (and the dormant venues and jobs). Only `server/lists.js` writes the locations and income types lists, and only `server/employees.js` writes employees. Only `server/shiftStore.js` writes shifts, their breaks, the employees on them, their parties, tags and money entries. Routes and scripts go through them.
9. **Validate at the door.** `server/validate.js` is pure (no I/O) and rejects unknown fields. The schema's CHECK constraints and foreign keys are a second line of defence (they cover the shape of a break and the allowed values; that breaks fit the shift, and rule 6, are the validator's and the store's job).
10. **Soft delete by default** (`deleted_at`); `?hard=1` purges and cascades. Referenced venues, jobs, locations, employees and income types are archived instead of deleted, so history keeps resolving.
11. **Locations and income types are pick lists, employees are a table, and shifts (or their income entries) point at all of them by id.** Renaming an entry renames it everywhere. A name is unique ignoring case, and adding a name that exists returns that entry (un-archiving it), so "add" is safe to repeat.

## Schema (v7)
`wage_rates` is one hourly-wage history: a rate applies from its `effective_from` (unique) until the next row. `venues` and `jobs` are dormant: nothing in the app creates them any more (the importer no longer does), and a shift's optional `job_id` is only kept if it already had one. `shifts` hang off an *optional* job (the form doesn't ask for one), an optional `locations` row, and own `shift_breaks`, `shift_employees` (→ `employees`), `parties`, `shift_tags` and `money_entries` (→ `income_categories`).

| table | what it holds |
|---|---|
| `shifts` | `work_date`, `start_at`, `end_at` (local wall-clock), `shift_type`, `job_id` and `location_id` (both optional), `notes`, `external_ref` (importer only), timestamps, `deleted_at` |
| `shift_breaks` | one row per break: `start_at` + `end_at`, or just `minutes` |
| `locations`, `income_categories` | `name` (unique ignoring case) and `archived`; the lists the form autofills from. `income_categories` also has `system`: the built-in Tips row |
| `employees` | `name` (unique ignoring case), `archived`, and the columns added since it was a plain list: `role` (free text) and `notes`. Add more here as the need comes |
| `shift_employees` | who worked a shift with you: `employee_id` plus optional `start_at` + `end_at` (both or neither, an end after its start) and `tips_cents`. One row per person per shift |
| `parties` | a party during a shift: `name`, `guests`, `start_at` + `end_at`, `notes`, every one optional. That the row exists is the flag; a shift can have several |
| `money_entries` | `category_id` (which income type), `value_cents`, and `part`: `day` or `night` (which half of a double), or `NULL` for combined / not sure |

`shifts.work_date` is the date you consider the shift to belong to (a 2am close belongs to the night before). It defaults to the date `start_at` falls on.

## API (`/api/v1`)
```
GET  /health                                   (open even when API_TOKEN is set)
GET|POST /wage-rates (one global history)     GET|PATCH|DELETE /<thing>/:id
GET|POST /venues, /jobs  (dormant)
GET|POST /locations, /income-categories, /employees   GET|PATCH|DELETE /<thing>/:id   (?include_archived=1 on the list)
GET  /employees/summary                        per-person shifts, hours and tips, derived on each read
GET  /shifts?from=&to=&job_id=&location_id=&employee_id=&has_party=1|0&limit=&cursor=&include_deleted=1   newest first, everything embedded
PUT  /shifts/:id        create or replace (201 / 200); the body carries money_entries[]
GET|PATCH /shifts/:id   PATCH merges then re-validates the whole document
DELETE /shifts/:id      soft delete; ?hard=1 purges
POST /shifts/:id/money  PATCH|DELETE /money/:id
GET  /export            every table, deleted shifts included
GET  /stats             raw row counts, schema version, server clock
GET  /events            Server-Sent Events stream (below)
```
A shift document (only `start_at`, `end_at` and `shift_type` are required):
```json
{ "start_at": "2026-09-18T11:00", "end_at": "2026-09-18T21:30", "shift_type": "double",
  "breaks": [ { "start_at": "2026-09-18T15:00", "end_at": "2026-09-18T15:30" }, { "minutes": 10 } ],
  "location_id": "…",
  "employees": [ { "employee_id": "…", "start_at": "2026-09-18T12:00", "end_at": "2026-09-18T20:00", "tips_cents": 9000 }, { "employee_id": "…" } ],
  "parties": [ { "name": "Smith 40th", "guests": 40 } ],
  "tags": ["busy"], "notes": "…",
  "money_entries": [ { "value_cents": 21000, "part": "day" },
                     { "value_cents": 34550, "part": "night" },
                     { "value_cents": 1000,  "part": null } ] }
```
A break is `{start_at, end_at}` or `{minutes}` (1 to 1440); read back, ranges come first in time order. `location_id` may be `null`, `employees`, `parties` and `breaks` may be empty, and `job_id` (still accepted, optional) is kept when a shift is edited. An unknown id is a 400. An income entry's `category_id` defaults to Tips (`00000000-0000-4000-8000-000000000001`); an unknown one is a 400. On a `day` or `night` shift, leave `part` out.

`employees` lists who else worked the shift. Each entry has an `employee_id` (which must exist) and, optionally, `start_at` and `end_at` (both or neither; the end after the start; no more than 24 hours; they may fall outside your own shift, since someone can arrive early or stay late) and `tips_cents`. The same person can't be listed twice. `parties` is a list of `{name, guests, start_at, end_at, notes}` where every field may be omitted or null: an empty `{}` is a party. Read back, both carry all their fields, nulls included, ordered by employee name and by start time.

`PATCH /employees/:id` changes `name`, `role` or `notes` (a blank role or notes becomes null; `409` if the name is taken). `POST /employees` is find-or-create by name, like the lists, and returns the existing person untouched (un-archived) rather than overwriting them.

`POST /locations`, `/income-categories` and `/employees` take `{name}` (employees also `role` and `notes`, used when the person is new) and are find-or-create: `201` when the entry is new, `200` (the existing one, un-archived if need be) when the name is already there. `PATCH` renames (`409` if the name is taken); `DELETE` answers `{archived: true}` when a shift uses the entry and `{archived: false}` when it was really deleted. The built-in Tips entry answers `409` to any change.

`GET /` serves the dashboard. It carries no data itself, so it needs no token. The browser may also load exactly these files, from a whitelist in `server/index.js`: the modules `/time.js` (this server's own `server/time.js`), `/pay.js`, `/form.js`, `/lists.js`, `/employees.js`, `/side.js`, `/viz.js`, `/insights.js`, `/tabs.js`, `/calendar.js` and `/overview.js`, the stylesheet `/styles.css`, and the four Poppins weights under `/fonts/`. The page is served with a strict CSP (`style-src 'self'`, `font-src 'self'`), so there are no inline styles and nothing is loaded from a third party. Nothing else on disk is reachable.
Errors are `{ "error": CODE, "problems": ["field: message", ...] }` with 400 validation, 401, 404, 405, 409 (unique or foreign-key conflict) or 413.

## Live feed
- **Stores emit, routes don't.** After a write commits, `catalog.js` and `shiftStore.js` call `emit` with `{entity, op, id, label, note?, data?}`. Failed writes emit nothing. `data` is the full record (a shift arrives with its tips and tags), so a dashboard can update without refetching. A hard delete sends only `id` (`op: purged`).
- **`GET /api/v1/events`** first sends `hello` (`{instance, recent[]}`; `instance` changes on every server start and `recent` is the last 100 events), then one `change` event per emit, with a ping every 15s. There is no replay by `Last-Event-ID`: a client that reconnects refetches a snapshot and dedupes by `instance:seq`.
- **Writes from other processes** (the importer, `sqlite3`) never pass through this process's stores. `createExternalWatch` polls `PRAGMA data_version`, which SQLite bumps only when a *different* connection commits, and emits `db.external` so open dashboards refetch.
- **The dashboard reads the stream with `fetch`**, not `EventSource`, because `EventSource` cannot send an `Authorization` header. It never builds markup from data (`textContent` only; a test forbids `innerHTML`), and the page is served with a strict CSP. When the table is rebuilt, keyboard focus is put back on the same row button.

## The shift form
- **It writes only through the public API**: `PUT /shifts/:id` with a client-made UUID (so a retried or double-clicked save can't duplicate), and `DELETE`, plus `POST /locations` / `POST /employees` for a name that isn't there yet. There is no dashboard-only write path, so every rule in `validate.js` and the schema still applies, and server rejections are shown in the form in plain words.
- **Tabbed navigation in section groups on the left, one page's detailed input on the right.** The shifts view is a workbench of two panels. The left one (`dashboard/side.js`, `#sidePanel`) is a vertical tab list in groups, and each tab is a page of its own: browsing has *Browse* (Overview, Calendar, Shifts, Live feed) and *Lists* (Locations, Misc types, Wage rates); editing has *Info* (Date, Time, Type), *Income* (Tips, Wage, Misc) and *Details* (Location, Crew, Party, Notes). The right one is that tab's pane, a full-width panel holding just that page's input. There is no popup and no long scroll: every page is one click away (or a Back / Next away, in the `STEPS` order) and the form's state is kept while another page is open. **The lists are not in the form at all**: locations, misc types and wage rates are edited only under Lists while browsing, so the form stays about the one shift (a location typed there that isn't on the list yet is still added on save). A tab shows a live one-line summary of its page (`paintSummaries`) and a red dot when a field on it is invalid (`syncFlags`), and a problem in a field on another page brings that page up (`revealProblem`). Panes are `role="tabpanel"` elements with `data-tab` (pane `paneX` for tab `x`); elements marked `data-only="edit"` (the form, Close, Save, Cancel, Delete) exist only while editing, and Save is a submit button bound to the form with `form="shiftForm"`. A group with no tab in the current mode is hidden, the tab stays when the mode changes if the new mode has it, and closing or saving lands on the browse page you came from. The shifts table has four data columns (date, time worked, tips, total income); each row's arrow opens a detail row (wage, other income, crew, party, breaks, notes, raw data), and month headings carry that month's totals (left off for the oldest loaded month while older shifts are unloaded, since it may be partial). The nav scrolls inside itself, with Save and Cancel fixed at its foot; on a phone the groups run together as one row of tabs across the top and the actions become a bar along the bottom.
- **Sections, in the order you fill them in:** date, time (start, end, breaks), shift type, then income in three tiers that mirror how it is thought of, **tips** (primary, entered), **estimated wage** (secondary, derived and read-only) and **other income** (third, entered, with a type), then location, party, employees, notes. There is no venue or job field; the form keeps a shift's existing `job_id` when editing (a shift needs none).
- **Typed as clock times, resolved by the shared helpers.** The form imports `server/time.js` through `/time.js`. An end at or before the start means the next day (`resolveEnd`); a break's start goes on the date nearest the shift (`resolveNearSpan`), so a break at 12:15 AM in a 5 PM to 1 AM shift lands on the next date.
- **Type** (the shift type) is a Day / Night / Double choice. It is required and not preselected.
- **Things that can be many are a strip of tabs, not a pile of rows** (`dashboard/tabs.js`): breaks, parties and the people on a shift each get a strip ("Break 1 · 30 min", "Smith 40th · 40 guests", "Leo · 8h · $40" and a "+ Add" tab) with one editor open at a time. Every editor stays built and only the open one is shown, so nothing typed is lost by switching; a field with a problem opens its own tab and dots it (`reveal`, `flag`). The panels inside are `role="tabpanel"` too, so anything that looks for a page's pane uses `[role="tabpanel"][data-tab]`.
- **Each page shows what it comes to, quietly** (`paintVisuals` in `form.js`): the form is for editing, so the figures are a line of `facts()` under the input, with an arrow (and words) where one is compared, never a chart. The Date page is a calendar of your logged shifts that says when the chosen day already holds one; Time shows worked, length and breaks with a slim **shift ribbon** (the shift along the clock, each break notched out; Crew and Party draw each person and party on the same thin track); Type shows what each type has averaged; Tips shows the rate against your average; Wage the total income; Location how the place has done. **The lists (locations, misc types, wage rates) are not edited in the form**, only under Lists while browsing. Only the page on show is redrawn on each keystroke. It is all derived from the shifts on the page with `derive` (pay.js with your wage history and roles), never stored.
- **Breaks** are a reusable list (`createBreakList` in `form.js`): "+ Add break" adds a tab, each break is a start/end pair or (switch) just a length in minutes, and each has a Remove. It returns `[]` when there are none, skips empty rows, and gives a clear message for a half-filled row, a break outside the shift, two that overlap, or breaks that together outlast the shift (the server checks the same, so the two can't disagree). Any other start/end range added later can mount its own list.
- **Tips and other income** are two groups over one list of entries (`ctx.entries`): an entry whose type is the built-in Tips is a tips row (no type to choose), any other is an other-income row (a type from your list, with Tips left out). Each row is [type,] (on a double) a Day / Night / *Combined* picker, the amount, a remove. New tips rows are Tips; on a double the first tips entry offers a row for the other half. If no other types exist the section says how to add one instead of offering an empty picker. Other types are managed under Edit types; tips and wage aren't types.
- **The estimated wage** is a read-only block, drawn as a result (tinted, dashed) rather than a field. It recomputes on every change to the date, times or breaks, using the same `pay.js` as the server, so it shows exactly what the API will report; a break row still being typed is ignored until it is valid. With no rate in effect on that date it says so and links to the wage tab.
- **Location and employees** are tap-to-pick pills (most recently worked first) plus a typed field with browser autocomplete. Location is one value; employees are any number. Each employee picked becomes a one-line row in the Crew page's list and a block of inputs beside it, built once and kept (so typed values survive a redraw), shown when that person is the one picked (the person just added is picked): their start, end and tips (all optional; times start as yours, and are placed on the dates nearest the shift with the same `resolveNearSpan`/`resolveEnd` as a break, so someone who stays past midnight lands on the next date) and their **role**. A role belongs to the person, not the shift, so for someone already on the table it is saved with `PATCH /employees/:id` as it is changed (as in the Employees tab) and the bartender figures follow; for a name that isn't on the table yet it is kept and sent with the `POST /employees` that creates them just before the shift is saved, so a retry after a failed save can't double-add. A name that was archived counts as new, except a shift's own unchanged location, which is kept as is so editing an old shift never resurrects a removed entry.
- **The list editors** (`dashboard/lists.js`) are built into every element marked `data-list` (a kind: `locations`, `income_categories` or `wage_rates`, and an optional `data-title`), so one implementation serves both places a list appears: on the page it feeds while a shift is open (Location, Misc, Wage) and on a page of its own in the Lists group while browsing. Locations and Misc types (the other-income kinds; Tips isn't shown, it isn't "other") are name lists: add, rename in place (Enter or leaving the field saves, Escape undoes), remove. Wage rates is the history, drawn as date ranges: each row is a start date and $/hr (edited in place; a second rate on the same date is refused with a plain message) with the range it covers underneath, through the day before the next rate starts or "from then on", and the range that applies to the shift being edited (`rateOn` from `pay.js`, on the form's date, passed through `onShiftDate`) is marked *This shift*. The form's "Set your hourly wage" takes the cursor to the Wage page's add row. Rows an editor changes go straight into the page's state, and changes made elsewhere arrive through the live feed (`changed()`), so the form's pills and the shift table stay current.
- **The Employees view** (`dashboard/employees.js`) is a page of its own (the *Employees* tab beside *Shifts*, `#employees` in the address), not a list, because employees are a real table with their own store and endpoints. It has two tiers. The **main panel** is the roster: one group per role (case-insensitive, "No role set" last) with the group's totals, then a row per person with their shifts, hours, tips and last shift. The **side panel** is whoever is picked: a **profile form** (name, role, notes; each field saved on its own through `PATCH /employees/:id`, Enter or leaving the field saves, Escape undoes, and it says whether the role counts as a bartender) over their **activity** (drawn as a tinted result, like the estimated wage: shifts, hours, tips, average per tipped shift, first and latest shift, and their latest eight shifts from `GET /shifts?employee_id=`), then a two-click Remove. The figures come from `GET /employees/summary` and are refetched when a shift changes. Changing a role re-computes every shift's bartender figures on the spot, since they are derived on each draw.
- **A live preview** under the wage block and under the employee rows recomputes the estimated wage, the bartender count and hours, and the staff tips as anything changes, using the same `pay.js` as the server.
- **Editing is lossless.** A work date that differs from the start date is preserved, tips keep their ids, and the job link survives.
- **Ids work over plain http** (a LAN or Tailscale address has no `crypto.randomUUID`); the form falls back to `getRandomValues`.

## Charts
- **Drawn with HTML in percentages, not a chart library** (`dashboard/viz.js`): bars, dots and lanes are positioned with `element.style` (the CSSOM, which the CSP allows; `setAttribute('style')` would be blocked), so a chart reflows with its card and there is no resize code. Names from the database only ever reach the page as text.
- **Rules every chart keeps:** one measure per axis (hours and income are two charts), bars at most 24 px thick with a 4 px rounded end and a 2 px gap between stacked parts, a legend wherever colour carries identity, the value at a bar's tip or the peak and last bars only, a tooltip on hover and on focus, and a *View as table* under each chart. The shift-type marks (`--mk-day`, `--mk-night`, `--mk-double`) were checked for colour-blind separation and contrast in light and dark; the income mix is one hue in three steps and is told apart by its labels.
- **Overview and Calendar** draw only while shown (`sidepaint` event) and are rebuilt from the shifts on the page, so they cover the shifts loaded (a note and *Load older shifts* say when older ones are not).

## The dashboard's look
- **One stylesheet, tokens first.** `dashboard/styles.css` starts with the tokens (colour for light and dark, a 4-px spacing scale, four type sizes plus title, radii, shadows) and everything below uses them, so a change to a colour, a size or a gap is made once.
- **Levels.** Depth has three: the page, panels (a hairline and a soft shadow), and the toast (raised). There are no dialogs: everything a shift points at is edited on the page of the form that uses it. Type has four: title, heading, an uppercase eyebrow (form sections, table headers), and body/label/small. Emphasis inside a level is weight, not size.
- **Poppins is self-hosted.** The four latin woff2 files (about 8 KB each) live in `dashboard/fonts/` with their licence and are served by the app, so it works offline or on a LAN, sends nothing to a third party, and keeps the CSP closed. `font-display: swap`, with a system stack as the fallback.
- **The table is one compact row per shift** (about 50 px; a card of about 160 px on a phone). Every cell is two short lines, the figure and then its detail, so the eye reads down a column: date (and, under it, the type badge, a Party badge when the shift had one, and the location), time worked (the hours lead; start → end is the detail, since how long you worked matters more than when), then Tips, Wage, Other and Total income (the amount, then its per-hour) and Bartenders (count · hours, then the staff tips). **Money is shown in whole dollars, rounded; the exact cents are in each cell's hover title**, which also carries the breakdown (which other-income types, the working of the wage, each employee's hours and tips). All of it is computed in the browser from the shift, the employees and the wage history with the shared `pay.js` each time a row is drawn, so a changed rate or role re-prices every row at once and the numbers match the API's `derived`. Detail that has no column (crew, tags, notes, party) is a hover title on the date. Clicking a row does nothing; only the icon buttons act.
- **Row actions are three SVG icon buttons on the right** (edit, raw JSON, delete), built with `createElementNS` since the page never assembles markup from strings. Delete is two clicks: the first arms that row for four seconds (the button turns red and reads *Delete?*, covering its two neighbours so no column moves), the second sends the soft delete. Rows that are already deleted offer only edit (which restores) and raw.
