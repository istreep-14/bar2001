Shift log: improvement blocks

Nine blocks that could be added to Shift › Shift log, each drawn with the app's own stylesheet and its real components, so what you are looking at is what the page would look like. The figures are made up and nothing clicks: these are pictures with notes, for deciding what is worth building. The CSS each block would add to dashboard/styles.css is in proposed.css beside this file, and nothing in the app has been changed.

01Filter bar: range, type, place, searchcore

Today
The panel head holds one control: Show deleted. Every shift that is loaded is on screen, newest first, and the only way to narrow it down is to scroll.
Change
A filter row under the panel head: a range, the shift type, the place, a text search over notes, tags, crew and party, and toggles for party nights, unfinished shifts and deleted ones. Under it, one chip per filter that is on, each taking itself off, and the count it left.
Why
Every question you ask the log is "of these shifts" — the Saturdays, the ones at the Harbour Room, the one with the wedding. The page already holds the shifts; the filters are a predicate in renderShifts() and a totals recount, the way #showDeleted already is.
Costs
dashboard/index.html (a state.filter object, the predicate, the chips) and the .logbar / .fchips rules. No server work: GET /shifts already takes from, to, location_id, employee_id and has_party, for when the filter should reach past what is loaded.

02Totals for the shifts on screencore

Today
Each month heading carries its own totals, and the Overview has totals for a range, but the log itself never says what the rows you are looking at add up to.
Change
A strip above the table, in the app's own tiles: shifts, hours, tips, the estimated wage, other income, and total income leading, each with its per-hour figure and its move against the range before it. It follows the filters, so it is always the total of what is on screen.
Why
It answers the question the filters are asked for. Filter to Saturdays at the Harbour Room and the strip tells you what Saturdays at the Harbour Room are worth, without going to the Overview and setting a range that cannot express it.
Costs
dashboard/insights.js already has summarize() and tipsPerHour/totalPerHour, and viz.js draws these tiles for the Overview. New CSS: the .logsum wrapper and .trend.

03Sortable columns and row densitycore

Today
The log is always newest first. The Employees roster next door already sorts by any column with .colsort and th[aria-sort].
Change
The same header buttons here, over the derived figures: hours, tips, tips/hr, wage, other, total, total/hr, crew. Sorting by a figure crosses the months, so the month headings give way to one line saying what the order is, with the way back in it. Two densities beside it, for reading a long stretch or for a phone.
Why
"Which nights were the best" is one click from a table that already computes every figure on every row. It is also the cheapest block here: the sort keys all exist on derive(s).
Costs
dashboard/index.html (renderShifts() sorts by a state.sort key and skips grouping when it isn't the date; the roster's handler can be copied). CSS: three lines for the header buttons, .sortnote, and the two density classes.

04Group headings that stick, fold and comparecore

Today
monthRow() draws a month's name and its totals, then scrolls away. Ten rows down a long month you no longer know which one you are in, and there is no way to skip a month or to see the shape of one.
Change
The heading sticks under the column header while its own shifts scroll past; a chevron folds the month away to one line; the income mix rides in it as the Overview's three-step bar; and the month's total carries its move against the month before.
Why
It is the log's only structure, and it is doing the least it could. Folding also makes a year of shifts navigable without a second control.
Costs
dashboard/index.html (monthRow() grows a chevron and a mix bar; a state.closedGroups set; grouping by week is one line in the key it groups by, which insights.js's mondayOf() already gives). CSS: the tr.group block, which replaces tr.month.

05Row states: unfinished, deleted, partycore

Today
A row says nothing about how complete it is, and a deleted row has to be opened in the form and saved to come back ("Save and restore"). A row with no date, no times or no income just shows dashes.
Change
Five states drawn once: a shift still to be finished (an amber dot after the date, the reason on hover), a deleted one with its own Restore where the bin was, the party badge as it is today, a row that just changed (the flash the live feed already triggers) and the armed delete.
Why
Leaving a shift half-filled is a feature — "fill in as little or as much as you know right now" — so the log needs to show which ones are waiting without calling them errors. And restoring something you deleted by accident should not be a trip through the form.
Costs
dashboard/index.html (shiftRow(): a flag when start_at/end_at or all income is missing, and a Restore button that re-PUTs the row the page already holds — shiftStore.put clears deleted_at, so no new endpoint). CSS: .flagdot, tr.shift.needs, .icon-btn.restore.

06The unfinished-shifts nudgecore

Today
Nothing tells you a shift you started logging on the way home was never finished. It sits in the table with dashes, and you find it by accident.
Change
One line above the table when any loaded shift is missing its times or its income, with a button that filters the log down to them (the Unfinished toggle from block 01) and a way to leave it alone.
Why
It is the one thing the log knows that you would want to be told, rather than have to look for. It is also what makes half-filled shifts safe to encourage.
Costs
dashboard/index.html (a count while rows are built, and a dismissal that lasts the session). CSS: .nudge.

07A table foot instead of a standing notecore

Today
Four sentences of legend sit under the table for good, above a full-width Load older shifts button, and nothing says what stretch of time is on screen or how much of it is unloaded.
Change
One foot line: how many shifts are loaded and the dates they cover, the caveat about a partial oldest month kept in plain sight, Load older as an ordinary button, and the legend folded into a <details> that remembers being open.
Why
The legend is read once; the "how far back does this go" question is asked every time, and the log cannot answer it today.
Costs
dashboard/index.html (the foot replaces #more and .tablenote; the oldest loaded date is already known — it is the paging cursor). CSS: .logfoot, .collegend.

What the columns mean
Hours
Time worked: the shift's length minus its breaks, which are unpaid.
Tips, Total
Whole dollars, with the per-hour rate beside them — green when it beat your average over the shifts on screen, amber when it fell short, and the bar showing where in their spread it sits.
Wage
Never entered: time worked × the hourly rate in effect that day.
Crew
Bartenders including you, and their hours together.
Anywhere
Hover a figure for the exact cents and how it was worked out.

08Empty, filtered-empty and loadingcore

Today
One line covers everything: "No shifts yet. Add your first with + New shift." A filter that matches nothing would show the same line, and while the first snapshot is in flight the table is simply blank.
Change
Three states, each saying what is actually going on and offering the way out of it.
Why
"No shifts" when you have 400 of them and a filter on is the kind of thing that makes you reload the page.
Costs
dashboard/index.html (three branches where #empty is toggled, and a flag for "no snapshot yet"). CSS: .empty-rich, .skel.

09Picking several rowsoptional

Today
Everything is one row at a time: delete, restore, tag, edit.
Change
A checkbox column, and a bar at the foot of the table's own scroller for what can be done to the picked rows: tag them, delete them, restore them, export just those.
Why, and why last
It earns its keep on a tidy-up (tagging a run of shifts at a place you have just named, restoring three you deleted by mistake) and on nothing else. It is the only block that adds a mode to a page whose rows currently do nothing when clicked, and it needs a PATCH per row, so it should only be built if the tidy-up turns out to be real.
Costs
dashboard/index.html (a selection set, the column, the bar, shift-click for a range). CSS: .pickcol, tr.shift.picked, .bulkbar.

If any of it ships

Order
03 (sorting and density) and 07 (the foot) are nearly free and change nothing else. 01 (filters) is the one everything else leans on — the totals strip, the empty states and the nudge all read the filter. 02, 04, 05, 06 and 08 are independent of each other once 01 exists. 09 is a maybe.
Where
All of it is dashboard/index.html's renderShifts(), shiftRow() and monthRow(), plus the rules in proposed.css folded into dashboard/styles.css under its shift-table section. No server change, no schema change, nothing stored: every figure here is already derived on each draw by pay.js and insights.js.
Rules kept
No markup built from strings (the page is textContent and createElement only, and a test enforces it), no inline style attributes (the CSP forbids them — hence the rate bar being SVG geometry, which blocks 02 and 04 follow with the mix bar's fixed steps), whole dollars in the table with the cents on hover, and every new colour taken from the tokens.
Then
README.md's dashboard section and ARCHITECTURE.md's "The dashboard's look" describe the table as it is today, down to the columns; whatever ships has to be written back into both.
